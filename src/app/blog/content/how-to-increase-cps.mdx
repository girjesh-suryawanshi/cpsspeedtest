## How to Increase Your CPS: From Beginner to Pro

So, you've taken a [CPS test](/cps-test) and your score is... well, let's just say it's not breaking any records. Don't sweat it! Every pro gamer started exactly where you are now. The good news is that increasing your Clicks Per Second (CPS) is a skill, and like any skill, you can train it.

When I first started playing Minecraft PvP, my CPS was stuck at around 6. I'd lose almost every fight. I thought fast clicking was just a natural talent some people had. I was wrong. With a bit of practice and the right techniques, you can see huge improvements.

Let's dive into how you can level up your clicking game.

### Method 1: The Foundation - Regular Clicking

Before you jump into the fancy stuff, master the basics. Regular clicking is just using your index finger to click as fast as you can.

-   **Get a Good Grip:** Don't hold your mouse in a death grip. Stay relaxed. A claw or fingertip grip usually works best.
-   **Use Your Arm, Not Just Your Wrist:** Small, controlled movements from your entire arm can be more stable and less tiring than just flicking your wrist.
-   **Practice in Bursts:** Don't try to click at max speed for minutes on end. Do short bursts of 5-10 seconds, rest, and repeat. Our [10-second test](/kohi-click-test) is perfect for this.

With practice, most people can get their regular clicking speed up to a respectable 7-9 CPS.

### Method 2: Jitter Clicking - The Power of Vibration

This is where things get interesting. **Jitter clicking** is about tensing your arm and hand to create a rapid vibration, which then translates into fast clicks.

It feels weird at first, like you're trying to control a tiny earthquake in your hand.

#### How to Jitter Click:
1.  **Position Your Hand:** Rest your hand on your mouse, but lift your wrist slightly off the pad.
2.  **Tense Up:** Tense your entire forearm and bicep. You should feel a tremor or vibration running down your arm to your finger.
3.  **Channel the Shake:** Let that vibration do the work. Your finger should rapidly tap the mouse button. You're not consciously clicking; you're directing the shake.

**Pros:** Can get you to 10-14 CPS. It looks cool.
**Cons:** It's tiring and can mess with your aim. Not great for long gaming sessions.

### Method 3: Butterfly Clicking - The Two-Finger Turbo

**Butterfly clicking** involves using two fingers (usually your index and middle finger) and alternating them on the same mouse button.

Imagine your two fingers are drumsticks, and the mouse button is the drum. Tap, tap, tap, in rapid succession.

#### How to Butterfly Click:
1.  **Position Your Fingers:** Place both your index and middle fingers on the left mouse button.
2.  **Find the Rhythm:** Start by slowly alternating clicks. Get a feel for the rhythm: index, middle, index, middle...
3.  **Speed It Up:** Gradually increase your speed. The goal is to make the clicks light and fast.

**Pros:** Insanely high CPS potential (15-20+). Less tiring than jittering once you master it.
**Cons:** Can be hard to aim. Requires a mouse that can handle double clicks without breaking (some mice have "debounce delay" to prevent this).

Check out our dedicated [Butterfly Click Test](/butterfly-click-test) to practice this technique!

### Method 4: Drag Clicking - The Ultimate Trick (with a Catch)

**Drag clicking** is the most advanced and controversial method. It involves literally dragging your finger across the mouse button to create friction, causing the switch to register dozens of clicks in a single swipe.

#### How to Drag Click:
1.  **Get the Right Mouse:** This is non-negotiable. You need a mouse with switches that support this (often mice with a grippy, matte surface work best).
2.  **The Motion:** Lightly place your finger on the very top edge of the mouse button.
3.  **Drag Down:** Gently drag your finger down the button. You should feel and hear a "grinding" or "vibrating" sound. Those are the clicks!

**Pros:** The highest possible CPS (can reach 30, 40, even 60+).
**Cons:** Wears out your mouse switch EXTREMELY fast. It's almost impossible to aim while doing it. Banned on many servers because it's considered almost like an autoclicker.

Ready to try it? Use our [Drag Clicking Test](/drag-click-test) to see what your mouse can do.

### A Quick Word on Safety: Don't Hurt Yourself!

This is super important. Trying to increase your CPS can put a lot of strain on your hand, wrist, and arm.

-   **Take Breaks:** Every 20-30 minutes, get up, stretch your hands and wrists.
-   **Don't Push Through Pain:** If you feel pain, STOP. Pain is your body telling you something is wrong. Rest.
-   **Stay Relaxed:** Tensing up too much is a recipe for Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI).

### Conclusion

Increasing your CPS is a marathon, not a sprint. Start with the basics, find a technique that feels comfortable for you, and practice consistently. Don't get discouraged if you can't jitter click on day one. I couldn't either! But with time, you'll see that number on the CPS test start to climb.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**1. How long does it take to get good at these techniques?**
It varies! Some people pick up butterfly clicking in a week, while jittering might take a month to feel natural. The key is consistent, short practice sessions every day.

**2. Can I increase my CPS without a gaming mouse?**
Yes, you can improve your regular clicking speed on any mouse. However, for advanced techniques like butterfly and drag clicking, a gaming mouse with sensitive switches is almost essential.

**3. Is 10 CPS considered fast?**
Yes, 10 CPS is very good! It's a solid benchmark for competitive gaming and puts you well above the average player.

**4. Which clicking method is the best?**
There's no single "best" method. Butterfly clicking often gives the best balance of speed and control for games like Minecraft, but it depends on your grip, your mouse, and what feels most comfortable for you.

**5. Does CPS matter in games other than Minecraft?**
It can! While Minecraft is the most famous example, having a fast click speed can also be helpful in other click-intensive games like MOBAs (for orb-walking), RTS games, and even some FPS titles for firing semi-automatic weapons quickly.
